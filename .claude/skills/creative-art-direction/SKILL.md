# Creative Art Direction

Use this skill to avoid generic template styling and create a brand-specific design language.

## Visual exploration
- Begin with purpose and audience, then choose one coherent creative direction rather than a collage of trends.
- Give the concept a specific identity: e.g. kinetic editorial, technical neo-brutalism, playful depth, refined product studio, futuristic control room.
- Pick a restrained palette with semantic roles (surface, elevated surface, foreground, muted, primary, accent, danger, success). Verify contrast on actual text.
- Establish a typography system: expressive display font, highly readable body font, scale, line height, letter spacing, and fallback fonts. Respect licensing and loading performance.
- Define spacing, radius, shadows, borders, grids, responsive breakpoints and layering as reusable tokens.

## Composition and storytelling
- Make the hero communicate a clear promise in the first viewport, with a useful CTA and a signature visual.
- Use visual hierarchy, purposeful negative space, asymmetry where appropriate, clean alignment and intentional rhythm.
- Introduce section transitions through changes in density, light, scale or motion; avoid repetitive interchangeable cards.
- Design real states: hover, focus, active, loading, error, success, disabled, empty, skeleton, dropdown, modal.
- On narrow screens, recompose the hierarchy instead of simply shrinking desktop elements.

## Design quality gate
- Is the concept instantly recognizable without relying on gradient overload?
- Is every element serving purpose, comprehension or emotion?
- Does the interface remain usable with images, 3D and animations disabled?
- Are headings readable, buttons obvious, and forms labeled?
- If references are requested, analyze them for principles; do not trace protected artwork or use unlicensed assets.

## Output
Provide concise art-direction notes and tokens, then implement them consistently in the project's current styling approach.
